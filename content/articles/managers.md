# The 3 types of managers I've seen

I've been working from the 2nd year of college(in limited scope during college). Twice at startups, once at an MNC, and once for GSOC for which I was given a stipend. Theoretically, I had 1.5 years of experience if you include internships post graduation. 

After graduation I was given a boon in joining a very amazing company, which I worked at for 6 months before leaving due to a conflict of my own personal passions. I think throughout my experiences I've met 3 different kind of managers I would report to, and what I think of these 3 types

1. The people leader
- very people oriented, very social
- Has his team's back, no matter what
- May or may not be technical it really depends
- Was insanely chill about stuff
- Got shit done from you regardless of being chill, since he had a good rapport with you, he could gently push you
- Could be incredibly stubborn at times about goals
- The teams success is his success.

2. The engineer
- typically a higher position engineer turned into a manager or CTO
- Results oriented(I don't care what blockers you have I could do it so can you)
- Sees team Engineers more as tools to accomplish goals(not to imply a lack of empathy however)
- Very direct(nothing wrong with this, I appreciate this!)
- Great to work under technically wise. You can appreciate their brilliance. Same cannot be said about their communication skills.
- The teams success is his success as well.

3. The lala manager:
- the word lala might be a corporate slang of sorts, but I had to work under one of these.
- Mostly confused about requirements
- Daily 180 degree turns about work
- Just horrid to work under.
- Can't say more or I'd be in trouble.

I appreciate type 1 and 2. Both have their pros and cons and it really comes down to the nature of work you are doing. I have worked in everything between firmware and frontend engineering so I have a slightly broader view of what work can feel like. 

I'm still quite young, but have worked for many orgs. I have passed over the OSS org for GSOC since that was a mentorship.
